Proposal for National ICT Acceleration and Redundancy: Bridge the Liberia Digital Divide (BLDD)


Executive Summary:

The Bridge the Liberia Digital Divide (BLDD) is a strategic acceleration plan to immediately achieve Liberia's Universal Access goals. It leverages Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite technology (Starlink) and Software-Defined Networking (SD-WAN) to provide resilient, high-speed internet to all 15 counties within months, bypassing the time-consuming and expensive terrestrial fiber rollout. 

This plan will serve as the guaranteed Redundancy and Acceleration Layer for the nation's existing fiber investments.

1. The Challenge and The Opportunity

Liberia's current national ICT plan, while vital, faces three critical challenges hindering rapid socio-economic development:

| Challenge | Impact on Liberia | BLDD Opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Fiber Deployment Speed | Years of delay before all 15 county capitals receive broadband access. | Immediate Access: Connect all 15 counties within 12 months. |
| Single Point of Failure (SPOF) | Total national internet dependency on a single physical fiber link. | Guaranteed Redundancy: Provide an independent, space-based backup link. |
| Power Grid Unreliability | Frequent power outages disrupt connectivity services nationwide. | Total Resilience: Ensure 24/7 internet service via integrated solar power. |
2. The BLDD Solution: A Hybrid, Resilient Network
The BLDD plan is a full-stack, standardized infrastructure model that can be replicated across all 15 counties. It is built on three pillars: Starlink Backhaul, SD-WAN Management, and Ubiquiti Last-Mile Distribution.
A. Central Hub Architecture
Each County Hub will be equipped with multiple Starlink Business Terminals and a central SD-WAN Appliance to aggregate bandwidth and manage traffic intelligently.
 * Core Function: The SD-WAN actively manages all incoming and outgoing traffic. In the long term, it will bond the existing fiber line with the Starlink links, prioritizing the fiber but instantly failing over to Starlink upon any physical fiber cut.

B. Last-Mile Distribution and Resilience
Connectivity is distributed from the County Hub to local communities, schools, and health clinics using proven, cost-effective wireless technology.
 * Distribution: High-performance wireless antennas (e.g., Ubiquiti Rocket Prism) transmit the Starlink-fed signal across the community. Homes, schools, and businesses use smaller, reliable Customer Premises Equipment (CPE) to receive the connection.
 * Power Assurance: Every Hub includes a dedicated Solar Power System to maintain 100% uptime, eliminating dependence on Liberia's electrical grid.

3. Strategic Value and Integration
The BLDD plan is designed to complement and accelerate existing national ICT initiatives:
 * National ICT Acceleration: BLDD instantly achieves the LTA's goal of connecting all county capitals years ahead of the fiber timeline, accelerating e-Government, e-Health, and e-Education initiatives nationwide.
 * Universal Access Fund (UAF): The BLDD provides a standardized, verifiable deployment model for the UAF. Funding can be efficiently channeled to acquire and deploy these ready-made Hub and Last-Mile Kits in the remotest districts.
 * Fiber Protection: The BLDD system safeguards the government's multi-million dollar investment in the terrestrial fiber backbone by providing the necessary Active-Passive Redundancy, ensuring Liberia never loses national connectivity.
